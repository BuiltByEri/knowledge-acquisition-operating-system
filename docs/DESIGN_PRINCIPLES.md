# KAOS Design Principles

## Principle 1 - AI Accelerates; Humans Decide

AI may assist with discovery, classification, comparison, reconciliation, drafting, and validation.

AI does not independently approve governance decisions.

Human review gates remain authoritative.

## Principle 2 - Canonical Knowledge Is Governed

A canonical entity is not created merely because a source record exists.

Canonical inclusion requires an explicit governed decision.

## Principle 3 - Provenance Is Preserved

Source origin, transformations, relationships, conflicts, and decision history must remain traceable.

Normalization must not erase source truth.

## Principle 4 - Source Conflict Is Preserved, Not Silently Flattened

Conflicting source claims must be represented, reviewed, and resolved or retained transparently.

The system must not invent consensus.

## Principle 5 - Acquisition Is Repeatable

The same lifecycle should produce consistent, reviewable outcomes when applied by different teams or to different domains.

## Principle 6 - Approval Boundaries Are Explicit

Planning, authorization, implementation, review, certification, and completion are separate responsibilities.

No stage may inherit authority that belongs to another stage.

## Principle 7 - Downstream Outputs Must Be Reproducible

Structured knowledge prepared for search, analytics, APIs, knowledge graphs, RAG, or AI applications must be traceable back to governed inputs and decisions.

## Principle 8 - The Framework Is Implementation-Agnostic

KAOS defines governance, lifecycle, artifacts, and decisions.

It does not require a specific database, AI model, programming language, cloud provider, vector store, or orchestration platform.
